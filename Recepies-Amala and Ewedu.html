Recipe for Amala and Ewedu

    Ingredients for Ewedu Soup

  • Ewedu leaves (jute leaves): 1 bunch (picked and washed)
  • Water: About 2 cups
  • Fermented locust beans (iru): 1 tablespoon
  • Ground crayfish: 1 tablespoon
  • Potash (kaun): A tiny pinch (optional, to soften the leaves and enhance the slimy "draw" texture)
  • Bouillon cube & Salt: For taste

    Ingredients for Amala

  • Yam/Plantain flour (Elubo): 2 cups
  • Hot Water: 2 cups (plus extra hot water for adjusting consistency)

Instructions

Ewedu Soup
  1. Rinse the plucked jute leaves thoroughly in clean water to remove any sand.
  2. Place a small pot on the stove, add the 2 cups of water and a tiny pinch of potash (if using), and bring it to a boil.
  3. Add the Ewedu leaves into the boiling water and cook for about 3 to 5 minutes until soft.
  4. Remove from heat and use a traditional broom whisk (ijabe) to mash the leaves smooth, or let it cool slightly and blend it briefly.
  5. Stir in the locust beans (iru), ground crayfish, bouillon cube, and salt. Let it simmer on low heat for 1 to 2 minutes, then set aside.
Amala
  1. Pour 2 cups of water into a medium pot and bring it to a rolling boil over medium-high heat.
  2. Reduce the heat slightly, then gradually pour the yam flour (elubo) into the boiling water while stirring continuously with a wooden spatula or paddle to prevent lumps.
  3. Turn off or lower the heat, add a little splash of hot water if needed, and cover the pot to let the Amala steam for about 3 to 5 minutes.
  4. Open the lid and stir vigorously until the Amala becomes smooth, soft, and stretchy.
  5. Shape into mounds using a wet spoon or bowl and serve hot alongside your Ewedu soup and a rich tomato-pepper beef or fish stew
